## How Amazon describes its interviews {#overview}
source: https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/interviewing-at-amazon
source: https://www.amazon.jobs/en/principles
source: https://amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/sde-ii-interview-prep
source: https://amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/product-manager-interview-prep

Amazon says its application and interview process differs from role to role, and it publishes separate interview prep guides by role. The guides reviewed for these notes (software development, product management, business intelligence, UX and university roles) all describe behavioral questions about how the candidate has demonstrated Amazon's Leadership Principles in past work. Role-specific notes apply only to the roles and levels they name.

## Leadership Principles {#leadership-principles}
tags: behavioral, leadership
source: https://www.amazon.jobs/en/principles

Amazon publishes 16 Leadership Principles:
- Customer Obsession
- Ownership
- Invent and Simplify
- Are Right, A Lot
- Learn and Be Curious
- Hire and Develop the Best
- Insist on the Highest Standards
- Think Big
- Bias for Action
- Frugality
- Earn Trust
- Dive Deep
- Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit
- Deliver Results
- Strive to be Earth's Best Employer
- Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility

Practice questions can ask for one specific past example that shows one of these principles.

## Student and new graduate SDE roles {#student-sde}
levels: intern, entry
roles: software-engineering
source: https://amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/university/sde-oa
source: https://amazon.jobs/content/en/career-programs/university/sde

For students and recent graduates applying to software development engineer (SDE) roles, Amazon describes an online assessment that evaluates coding skills, technical problem solving and its Leadership Principles. For internships it has a coding assessment (about 70 minutes), a workstyles assessment (about 15 minutes) and a feedback survey, about 90 minutes in total; full-time roles add a work simulation of about 60 minutes. Candidates who move forward are invited to interview, and Amazon says interviewers ask about past experiences and specific situations to assess potential performance.

## Student and new graduate data roles {#student-data}
levels: intern, entry
roles: data-analytics
source: https://amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/university/data

For student and graduate Business Intelligence Engineer and Data Engineer roles, Amazon describes one round of two interviews for internships, mixing technical and behavioral questions, and two rounds for full-time roles (one interview, then three to four), each 45 to 60 minutes. Technical interviews combine verbal and coding exercises, and Amazon says BIEs need an understanding of statistics and proficiency in SQL. For behavioral questions on the Leadership Principles, each interviewer typically asks two or three, Amazon suggests the STAR method, and it says it does not ask brain teasers.

## Student and new graduate technical roles {#student-technical}
levels: intern, entry
roles: it-cybersecurity
source: https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/university/additional-tech

Amazon's prep page for student and graduate technical roles such as quality assurance engineer, security engineer and system development engineer describes one round of two interviews for internships and two rounds for full-time roles (the second with three to four interviews), each 45 to 60 minutes. A significant part of each conversation covers how the candidate has demonstrated the Leadership Principles; interviewers typically ask two or three such questions, Amazon suggests the STAR method, and it says it does not ask brain teasers.

## SDE II roles {#sde-ii}
levels: mid
roles: software-engineering
tags: technical
source: https://amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/sde-ii-interview-prep

For SDE II roles, Amazon describes an online assessment with two technical questions in 90 minutes, 20 minutes of systems design scenarios and an 8-minute work style survey, followed by a loop of four 55-minute interviews. Amazon says candidates should write syntactically correct code (no pseudocode), expect at least one software systems design question, and answer behavioral questions about the Leadership Principles using the STAR method, with metrics or data where applicable.

## SDE III roles {#sde-iii}
levels: senior
roles: software-engineering
tags: technical, system-design
source: https://amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/sde-iii-interview-prep

For SDE III (senior software development engineer) roles, Amazon says the 60-minute technical phone screen is with a senior leader and splits its time evenly between Leadership Principles questions and coding and system design. The loop has five 55-minute interviews, and candidates should expect at least one software systems design question. Amazon recommends the STAR method and says answers should include metrics or data where applicable.

## Software development manager roles {#sdm}
levels: lead
roles: software-engineering
tags: system-design, leadership
source: https://amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/sdm-interview-prep

For software development manager (SDM) roles, Amazon says the 60-minute technical phone screen with a senior leader splits its time between behavioral questions on the Leadership Principles and system design questions about new or existing systems. The loop has five 55-minute interviews covering technical and non-technical competencies, and Amazon describes the role as motivating, mentoring and managing engineering teams. It recommends the STAR method, with metrics or data where applicable.

## Product manager roles {#product-manager}
levels: mid, senior, lead
roles: product-management
source: https://amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/product-manager-interview-prep

For product manager roles, Amazon says the 60-minute phone screen with a senior leader splits its time between behavioral questions on the Leadership Principles and functional questions about product management experience. The loop has five 55-minute interviews with members of its product management community, and the listed competencies include product management, stakeholder management and a writing exercise. Amazon recommends the STAR method, with metrics or data where applicable.

## Business intelligence engineer roles {#bie}
levels: mid, senior, lead
roles: data-analytics
source: https://amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/bie-interview-prep

For business intelligence engineer (BIE) roles, Amazon describes an online assessment with a SQL challenge, a working-with-SQL section and a work styles section, a 60-minute technical phone screen with a senior leader, and a loop of five 55-minute interviews. Listed topics include SQL and basic scripting, analytical problem solving, visualization, metrics and reporting, and business acumen and requirements gathering. Amazon recommends the STAR method, with metrics or data where applicable.

## UX designer and researcher roles {#ux}
levels: mid, senior, lead
roles: ux-design
source: https://amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/ux-interview-prep

For UX designer and researcher roles, Amazon says the loop includes a portfolio review, an interactive functional exercise (for example a design whiteboard or research planning exercise) and one-to-one behavioral interviews focused on the Leadership Principles. Each session is 60 minutes.

## Amazon Web Services hiring {#aws}
names: amazon web services, aws
source: https://aws.amazon.com/careers/how-we-hire/

AWS describes four steps in its hiring process for technical roles: online application, assessments, phone interview and in-person interviews. It advises candidates to consider their own successes and failures in relation to the Leadership Principles before interviewing and to plan to describe their experiences using the STAR method. AWS interviews include a bar raiser, described as an objective third party who focuses on long-term vision rather than the immediate hiring need.
