## How Microsoft describes its interviews {#overview}
source: https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips
source: https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips/interview-tips.html

Microsoft says most interviews include two to four conversations with potential teammates and cross-functional colleagues, each up to an hour, held by phone, on Microsoft Teams or in person, sometimes after a brief screening conversation. For some roles candidates may be asked to write code, share a creative portfolio or provide other work samples. Microsoft asks candidates to prepare specific examples from past experience related to the role and recommends the STAR(R) model: situation, task, action, result and reflection.

## Students and early-career candidates {#early-career}
levels: intern, entry
source: https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips/student-interviewing

For students and early-career candidates, Microsoft describes an initial interview and, if selected, a final interview, all held virtually. The process varies by role: some roles ask candidates to write code or share a portfolio or project, and candidates should prepare for both behavioral and functional or technical questions. A virtual interview day consists of one-to-one meetings with Microsoft employees, with breaks between interviews.

## Technical interviews {#technical}
roles: software-engineering
tags: technical, system-design
source: https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips/technical-interviewing

Microsoft's technical interviewing guidance describes 45-minute interview rounds. It asks candidates to ask clarifying questions, plan before implementing, and write clean, concise, bug-free code, and says interviewers want to see how candidates break down information and which algorithms they use. Listed data structures include arrays and strings, queues and lists, linked lists, trees and tries, hash maps and hash sets, and graphs; for system design it calls knowledge of distributed systems, SOA and n-tiered architecture very important.
