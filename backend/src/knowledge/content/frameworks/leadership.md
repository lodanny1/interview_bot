## How leadership interviews work {#overview}

Leadership questions are usually behavioral questions about influence rather than authority: setting direction, making decisions with incomplete information, resolving conflict, taking ownership of a problem, and helping others grow. Candidates without a management title can answer with examples from projects, student organizations, internships or team assignments where they led part of the work.

## What a strong leadership answer shows {#strong-answers}
tags: leadership, behavioral

A strong answer explains the goal, the people involved and their different views, how the candidate built agreement or made the call, and what changed as a result. Interviewers look for ownership of mistakes, credit given to others, decisions backed by data or clear reasoning, and follow-through after the decision.

## Expectations for senior and lead candidates {#senior-lead}
levels: senior, lead
tags: leadership

At senior and lead levels, interviewers expect examples with a larger scope: leading a team or a cross-team effort, hiring and developing people, handling underperformance, managing up, and making tradeoffs between delivery, quality and team health. They often ask how the candidate measured the team's success.
