## How technical interviews work {#overview}

Technical interviews check whether a candidate can apply role-specific skills to a concrete problem: writing code, querying data, debugging, or explaining how a tool or concept works. The interviewer usually cares about the process as much as the final answer. Formats include live problem solving, take-home exercises discussed in the interview, and conceptual questions about past technical work.

## What a strong technical answer shows {#strong-answers}
tags: technical

Strong candidates restate the problem and ask clarifying questions before starting, talk through their approach and its tradeoffs, start with a simple working solution and then improve it, test their work with normal and edge-case inputs, and say plainly when they are unsure. When asked about past projects, they explain their own decisions, the alternatives they considered and the measurable effect of the work.

## Interviewer follow-ups {#follow-ups}
tags: technical

Follow-ups often change one constraint to see how the candidate adapts: larger inputs, tighter time or memory limits, unreliable inputs, or a new requirement. Interviewers also ask why one approach was chosen over another, what it costs in time and space, and how the candidate would test or monitor the solution in production.
