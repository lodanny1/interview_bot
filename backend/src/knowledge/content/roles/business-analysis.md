## What business and financial analyst interviews assess {#overview}

Business and financial analyst interviews usually assess structured problem solving with numbers, comfort with spreadsheets and basic financial or business concepts, the ability to gather requirements from stakeholders, and clear recommendations. Many processes include a case or modeling exercise alongside behavioral questions.

## Early-career expectations {#early-career}
levels: intern, entry

For interns and entry-level analysts, interviewers check that the candidate can work through a small case or calculation without losing track of the numbers, explain basic concepts such as revenue, cost and margin or reading a financial statement, and communicate a recommendation simply. Coursework, case competitions and internships are valid examples.

## Mid-level expectations {#mid-level}
levels: mid

Mid-level analysts are expected to own analyses and processes: build forecasts or models, explain variances, translate stakeholder needs into requirements, and recommend changes backed by data. Interviewers often ask about a recommendation that was challenged and how the candidate defended or revised it.

## Senior and lead expectations {#senior-lead}
levels: senior, lead
tags: leadership

Senior and lead analysts are expected to shape planning or business decisions across teams, set modeling and reporting standards, manage stakeholders with conflicting goals, and develop other analysts. Interviewers probe the business impact of past work.

## Case and modeling exercises {#case-exercises}
tags: technical

Case exercises present a business question, such as whether to launch a product or why profit fell, and look for a clear structure, stated assumptions, careful math and a recommendation with risks and next steps. Modeling exercises check spreadsheet logic, sanity checks and how the candidate explains results to a non-specialist.
