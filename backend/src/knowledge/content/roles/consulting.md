## What consulting interviews assess {#overview}

Consulting interviews usually assess structured problem solving, comfort with numbers, clear communication, and the ability to work with clients and teams. Most processes combine case interviews with behavioral or fit questions about leadership, teamwork and motivation for consulting.

## Early-career expectations {#early-career}
levels: intern, entry

For interns and entry-level consultants, interviewers look for a logical structure, sensible assumptions, accurate mental math and a clear recommendation in case questions, plus examples of teamwork and initiative from school, work or activities. Candidates are not expected to know a client's industry in depth.

## Mid-level expectations {#mid-level}
levels: mid

Mid-level consultants are expected to own workstreams: plan analyses, manage client conversations, synthesize findings into recommendations and coach junior team members. Interviewers often ask about a difficult client situation and how the candidate kept the work on track.

## Senior and lead expectations {#senior-lead}
levels: senior, lead
tags: leadership

Senior and lead consultants are expected to manage engagements and client relationships, shape proposals, and lead teams. Interviewers probe the outcomes delivered for clients, how the candidate handled scope changes or conflict, and how they developed people.

## Case interviews {#case-interviews}
tags: technical

A case interview gives a business problem, such as declining profits or entering a new market. Strong candidates clarify the goal, lay out a structure, state a hypothesis, ask for or estimate the data they need, do the math out loud, and finish with a recommendation, risks and next steps. Interviewers care more about the reasoning than one right answer.
