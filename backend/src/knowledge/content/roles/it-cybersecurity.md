## What IT and security interviews assess {#overview}

IT support, systems and cybersecurity interviews usually assess structured troubleshooting, understanding of operating systems, networks and security basics, clear communication with non-technical users, and judgment under pressure. Many processes mix scenario questions ("a user cannot reach a site, what do you check?") with behavioral questions about incidents and customer service.

## Early-career expectations {#early-career}
levels: intern, entry

For interns and entry-level candidates, interviewers check fundamentals such as how DNS, DHCP and TCP/IP work, common Windows and Linux administration tasks, and basic security concepts like least privilege and phishing. Home labs, certifications, coursework and help desk or campus IT experience are valid examples when the candidate can explain what they did step by step.

## Mid-level expectations {#mid-level}
levels: mid

Mid-level candidates are expected to own systems or investigations: diagnose recurring problems, automate routine work with scripts, harden configurations, and handle incidents from detection to follow-up. Interviewers often ask about an outage or security incident the candidate worked and what changed afterwards.

## Senior and lead expectations {#senior-lead}
levels: senior, lead
tags: leadership

Senior and lead candidates are expected to design infrastructure or security programs, prioritize risks, lead incident response, set standards and mentor others. Interviewers probe how the candidate balanced security with usability and cost, and how they communicated risk to leaders.

## Scenario and troubleshooting questions {#scenarios}
tags: technical

Scenario questions describe a problem, such as a slow network, a locked account or a suspicious login, and watch how the candidate narrows it down: asking what changed, checking the simplest causes first, gathering evidence from logs, and explaining each step. Good answers say how the candidate would confirm the fix and prevent a repeat.
