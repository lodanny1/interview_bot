## What product management interviews assess {#overview}

Product management interviews usually assess product sense (understanding users and choosing what to build), analytical thinking with metrics, execution and prioritization, communication, and working across engineering, design and business teams. Common formats are product design questions, metrics and estimation questions, and behavioral questions about past launches.

## Early-career expectations {#early-career}
levels: intern, entry

For interns and associate product managers, interviewers look for structured thinking, curiosity about users, comfort with basic metrics, and evidence of getting things done with others, for example a class project, startup, club or internship where the candidate defined a problem and shipped something.

## Mid-level expectations {#mid-level}
levels: mid

Mid-level product managers are expected to own a product area: write clear requirements, prioritize a backlog with tradeoffs, define success metrics before launch, and work through disagreements with engineering and design. Interviewers often ask about a launch that went wrong and what changed afterwards.

## Senior and lead expectations {#senior-lead}
levels: senior, lead
tags: leadership

Senior and lead product managers are expected to set product strategy across teams, make portfolio tradeoffs, align executives, and coach other product managers. Interviewers probe the business outcomes of past decisions and how the candidate handled conflicting priorities between teams.

## Product design and metrics questions {#product-questions}

Product design questions ask the candidate to improve or create a product for a group of users; strong answers identify the users and their needs, prioritize problems, propose solutions and define how success would be measured. Metrics questions ask how to measure a feature or why a metric moved, and reward clear definitions and sensible follow-up checks.
