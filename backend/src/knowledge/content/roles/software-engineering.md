## What software engineering interviews assess {#overview}

Software engineering interviews usually assess problem solving and coding, understanding of core computer science concepts, the ability to explain technical decisions, and collaboration. Most processes combine coding exercises with behavioral questions about past projects; design questions matter more as seniority increases.

## Early-career expectations {#early-career}
levels: intern, entry

For interns and entry-level engineers, interviewers focus on fundamentals: writing correct, readable code, using common data structures, reasoning about time complexity, and testing. Class projects, internships, hackathons and personal projects are all valid examples. Interviewers look for curiosity, openness to feedback, and clear explanations of what the candidate personally built.

## Mid-level expectations {#mid-level}
levels: mid

Mid-level engineers are expected to own features end to end: break down requirements, make design choices within a service, write tests, review code, and handle production issues. Behavioral questions often ask about a difficult bug, a tradeoff between speed and quality, or working with product and design partners.

## Senior and lead expectations {#senior-lead}
levels: senior, lead
tags: system-design, leadership

Senior and lead engineers are expected to set technical direction for a team: design systems that span services, weigh build-versus-buy and migration decisions, raise the quality bar through reviews and standards, mentor other engineers, and influence roadmaps. Interviewers probe the scope and impact of past work and how the candidate handled technical disagreements.

## Coding exercises {#coding-exercises}
tags: technical

Coding rounds typically give a problem to solve in a shared editor or on a whiteboard. Common topics are arrays and strings, hash maps, trees and graphs, recursion, sorting and searching. Interviewers watch whether the candidate clarifies the problem, explains an approach before coding, handles edge cases and checks the code by walking through an example.
