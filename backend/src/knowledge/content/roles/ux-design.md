## What UX interviews assess {#overview}

UX design and research interviews usually assess how the candidate understands users, frames problems, makes and defends design or research decisions, and works with product managers and engineers. Most processes include a portfolio or case study presentation, a design or research exercise, and behavioral questions about collaboration and feedback.

## Early-career expectations {#early-career}
levels: intern, entry

For interns and entry-level candidates, interviewers look for a clear process in two or three portfolio projects, including class or personal work: the problem, the users, the research or testing done, the options considered and why the final design was chosen. Honest discussion of what the candidate would change matters more than polished visuals.

## Mid-level expectations {#mid-level}
levels: mid

Mid-level designers and researchers are expected to own features or studies end to end: plan research, turn findings into design decisions, handle trade-offs with engineering constraints, and measure whether a design worked. Interviewers often ask about a time research changed a product decision or a design was pushed back on.

## Senior and lead expectations {#senior-lead}
levels: senior, lead
tags: leadership

Senior and lead candidates are expected to shape product direction through design and research, build or maintain design systems and research practices, mentor other designers or researchers, and influence leaders. Interviewers probe the scope of past work and how the candidate aligned teams with different priorities.

## Portfolio reviews and design exercises {#portfolio-and-exercises}
tags: technical

In a portfolio review the candidate walks through a few projects; interviewers interrupt to ask about decisions, constraints and results. Design or research exercises give an open prompt, such as improving a flow or planning a study, and look at whether the candidate clarifies the user and goal, explores options before converging, and explains how they would test the result.
